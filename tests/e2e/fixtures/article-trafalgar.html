The Battle of Trafalgar was a naval engagement fought on 21 October 1805 between the Royal Navy and the combined fleets of France and Spain during the Napoleonic Wars.[1] Twenty-seven British ships of the line under Vice-Admiral Horatio Nelson defeated thirty-three French and Spanish ships under Admiral Villeneuve.

Nelson was shot by a French musketeer and died shortly before the action ended. The victory confirmed the naval supremacy Britain had held since the previous century and ended Napoleon's plans to invade England.

Background[edit]

In 1805 Napoleon planned an invasion of Britain that depended on control of the Channel for a matter of days. The strategy required the combined fleet to draw the blockading squadrons away to the West Indies and return before they could follow.

  • March 1805 — Villeneuve escapes the blockade of Toulon.
  • July 1805 — the indecisive action off Cape Finisterre.
  • September 1805 — Nelson resumes command off Cádiz.

Contemporary accounts are collected at the National Maritime Museum, which holds the fleet's signal logs.

The battleedit

Nelson attacked in two columns perpendicular to the enemy line, accepting raking fire during the approach in exchange for a melee his better-trained crews would win.

The Victory broke the line astern of the Bucentaure at about 12:45.

Aftermath

The storm that followed the battle cost both fleets more ships than the fighting had. Nelson's body was returned to England preserved in brandy, and he was buried in St Paul's Cathedral in January 1806.
